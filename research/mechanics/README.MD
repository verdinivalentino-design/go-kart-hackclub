# 1 - ⚙️ Kart Mechanics

First, we have to make it move, right? Well, I will apply my knowledge of clutches and ball bearings, while learning more about the steering system, brakes, suspension, chassis design, and more. At the moment, I am designing the chassis in Solid Edge and learning this software through a course I am taking at the local [UTN - Universidad Tecnológica Nacional](https://www.frsr.utn.edu.ar/grupos-de-extension/), the largest engineering university in my country.

This mechanical setup is quite large and costly, but I have access to many of the necessary tools to weld, grind, and measure through my school (where I am authorized), teachers, and friends. I only need the raw materials like pipes, the motor, clutch, and other components listed in the BOM Budget (Bill of Materials).

The Kart structure will be something similar to a **buggy** model. This is because I need enough space to fit the motor, the gasifier, and any future modifications. I am designing it with Solid Edge, a very useful CAD software for mechanical engineering. At home, I have an arc welder and an angle grinder. I learned to use them at school and earlier with my dad, and I can count on his helping hands for this project as well. At school, I am also allowed to use the pipe bender we have in the workshop.

The motor I will use is taken from a 110cc motorcycle—very common in my country—which means cheap spare parts are widely available, and some can even be found for free. The engine must be a **4-Stroke Engine**, because biomass combustion would work poorly on a 2-stroke engine (since wood gas lacks the necessary oil lubrication). Additionally, 4-stroke engines offer better valve control to prevent exhaust gas leaks.

Motorcycle engines include an alternator to power our sensors and the ESP32 (via voltage regulation), as well as a centrifugal clutch and gearbox. For this project, I am planning to build a manual lever system to shift gears.

At the beginning of the project, I wasn't planning to add suspension. However, one of my teachers encouraged me to dive into it, and I found two common types that I will use for the front and rear wheels. For the rear wheels, I will build a Trailing-Arm setup, and for the front wheels, a Double-Wishbone suspension. The only parts I will buy or source are the bolts, coil springs, and shock absorbers—likely salvaged from old motorcycles on second-hand platforms like Facebook Marketplace.
